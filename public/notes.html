📘 Notes: The 3 Kinds of Flashback

A flashback takes the reader from the present back to a memory and (usually) back again. A trigger (a sound, smell, object or feeling) starts it. A time clue ("Last summer…") tells the reader we have moved. Begin the memory with past perfect (had) and then use simple past.

← Back to the stories

1. Classical Flashback

Shape: Present (para 1) → long memory (paras 2–5) → back to present (last para).

Purpose: the memory IS the main story.

Use it when: a past event is the heart of the story.

Example start: Mei pressed the faded bell, and she was seven years old again.

2. Classic Linked Flashback

Shape: Present → memory that teaches a lesson → a similar situation in the present (second-last para) → the character acts differently (last para).

Purpose: show growth. The memory changes what the character does.

Example link: The boy held his lunch box tightly, just as Ravi once had.

3. Embedded Linked Flashback

Shape: The story moves forward as normal, with a short memory (1–2 paragraphs) placed inside the action.

Purpose: quickly explain why the character feels or acts that way.

Remember: keep it short, place it at an emotional moment, explain (don't just describe), then carry on with the present story.

Example: The puppy's warmth reminded Tom of Max, who had taught him to always help animals in need.
